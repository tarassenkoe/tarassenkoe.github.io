Kaggle Portfolio Project

Telecom Customer Churn & Data Quality Analysis

A practical data-analysis project designed to identify churn patterns, validate data quality, and translate customer behavior into retention recommendations.

DomainTelecommunications
Primary GoalUnderstand Churn
Data FocusQuality + Customer Behavior
ToolsPython / SQL / BI

Business Problem

Customer churn directly affects recurring revenue and customer lifetime value. The objective of this analysis is to determine which customer characteristics and service patterns are associated with churn, while first ensuring that the underlying data is complete, consistent, and analytically reliable.

Analysis Workflow

01

Data Quality Audit

Check missing values, duplicate rows, inconsistent categories, invalid ranges, unusual values, and datatype problems.

02

Exploratory Analysis

Profile customer tenure, charges, services, contracts, demographics, usage patterns, and churn distribution.

03

Segmentation

Compare churn across customer groups such as contract type, tenure bands, service combinations, and payment behavior.

04

Correlation Analysis

Measure relationships among numeric variables and use a heatmap to identify potentially important drivers.

05

Churn Drivers

Identify variables most strongly associated with churn and distinguish useful business signals from simple correlation.

06

Recommendations

Translate findings into retention actions, customer targeting ideas, KPI monitoring, and data-quality improvements.

Key Questions

Which customer segments have the highest churn rate?

Does tenure meaningfully change churn risk?

How do monthly or total charges relate to churn?

Which contract or service combinations are associated with greater retention?

Are missing values, outliers, or inconsistent fields distorting the analysis?

What customer groups should a retention team prioritize?

Correlation Heatmap

The heatmap can use the Pearson correlation coefficient:

r = Σ[(xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)] / √(Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² · Σ(yᵢ − ȳ)²)

A value near +1 indicates a strong positive linear relationship, near −1 a strong negative relationship, and near 0 little linear relationship. Correlation is useful for exploration but does not by itself prove causation.
